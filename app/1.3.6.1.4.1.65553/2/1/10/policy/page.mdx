## Scope and Protocol Field

The identifiers below are stable certificate-policy semantics for a privately distributed trust domain. They are encoded as `certificatePolicies.policyIdentifier` in X.509 certificates. They do not assert CA/B Forum DV/OV/EV status, public root-program membership, hardware-backed keys, or certification under a government identity-card scheme.

Root and issuing CA keys are software keys managed by EJBCA. The G6 operating parameters authorized for this deployment are 15-year roots, 5-year issuing CAs and 2-year subscriber certificate profiles, expressed using EJBCA relative validity notation. Status distribution uses CRLs; OCSP is not configured. Certificate expiry remains bounded by the issuer's expiry.

## Issuance and Enforcement

Issuing CAs and profiles are separated into TLS, S/MIME, code signing, person authentication, device authentication and timestamp signing. Every subscriber certificate carries the policy applicable to its actual authorized use. Applications must validate the chain, identity/SAN, KU/EKU, certificate time and applicable revocation information; the presence of an OID alone is not proof of compliance.

The operator verifies the authorization and identity evidence described by the selected identifier before enrollment. DN, KU, extension, validity and serial-number overrides are disabled in the corresponding G6 certificate profiles. Root and subordinate certificates use critical CA Basic Constraints and keyCertSign/cRLSign; subscriber certificates must have CA=false and no path length constraint.

Mail signing and mail encryption use distinct keys and certificates. Code signing certificates do not assert platform distribution approval. Timestamp signer certificates have only the critical timeStamping EKU; operating a real RFC 3161 endpoint requires a separately deployed TSA and configured timestamp-token policy.

These identifiers do not promise key escrow, recovery services or a particular hardware protection level. A new document revision may clarify unchanged semantics, while material changes to the identity or assurance promise require a new identifier. OIDs remain permanently allocated after a profile or key is retired.
