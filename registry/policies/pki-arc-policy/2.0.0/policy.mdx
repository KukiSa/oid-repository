# 1.3.6.1.4.1.65553.2.0 – Youngdo PKI Arc Policy

## Document Control

- **Policy reference:** `POL-PKI-2.0.0`
- **Revision:** 2.0.0
- **Status:** Effective
- **Published and effective:** September 10, 2026
- **Supersedes:** `POL-PKI-1.0.0`
- **Delegation:** `DLG-2026-0001`
- **Registration authority:** Youngdo PKI Registration Authority

## 1. Scope

This policy governs allocations under `1.3.6.1.4.1.65553.2` for PKI and trust services operated by Youngdo Technologies Limited. It is subordinate to `POL-ROOT-2.0.0` and to the limits of delegation `DLG-2026-0001`.

No allocation is authorized merely because a numeric value is unused. Every allocation must appear in the structured registry with an approved decision and this or a more specific effective policy.

## 2. Allocated Sub-Arcs

The structured registry authorizes these immediate sub-arcs:

- `1.3.6.1.4.1.65553.2.1` — stable Certificate Policy identifiers;
- `1.3.6.1.4.1.65553.2.2` — individual trust-anchor identifiers;
- `1.3.6.1.4.1.65553.2.3` — explicitly defined validation-profile identifiers;
- `1.3.6.1.4.1.65553.2.4` — private-use identifiers, without any implication of confidentiality;
- `1.3.6.1.4.1.65553.2.5` — immutable, versioned trust-anchor groups.

The policy-document node `1.3.6.1.4.1.65553.2.0` is retained as a legacy allocation identifying this policy family. Other immediate values remain unallocated.

## 3. Certificate Policies and Practice Documents

An OID under `.2.1` identifies stable certificate-policy semantics, not a mutable document edition. Each CP or CPS edition must have its own immutable document revision and effective time. A CPS document may be linked to a policy OID without receiving a separate OID unless a registration decision explicitly requires one.

## 4. Trust Anchors

Individual trust anchors under `.2.2` are governed by `POL-TA-2.0.0`. A trust-anchor OID must be bound to an exact public key and associated trust data before its operational state may become `active`.

Trust-anchor groups are allocated under `.2.5`, separately from individual anchors. Each group is an arc, and each immutable version appends a non-negative integer version component. Version `0` is valid. An existing group version must not be redefined; membership changes require a new version.

## 5. Validation Profiles

Before any allocation under `.2.3`, a subordinate policy must define the profile's machine-readable semantics, intended protocol field, conformance requirements, and relationship to certificate-policy OIDs. A marketing label or assurance claim alone is insufficient.

## 6. Private Use

Allocations under `.2.4` remain globally unique OIDs even when their semantics are private. The word “private” does not imply that published names, values, or protocol use are confidential.

## 7. Decisions, Incidents, and Audit

New allocations, identity changes, policy changes, and delegation changes require registration decisions. Trust-anchor compromise must result in an object lifecycle event and, when a replacement key is introduced, a new OID. The compromised OID remains permanently registered.

The Registration Authority must periodically reconcile registry data against authoritative PKI ceremony records and published cryptographic artifacts. Unknown legacy fields must remain marked unverified until that reconciliation is completed.

*End of Policy.*
