# 1.3.6.1.4.1.65553.2.2.0 – Individual Trust Anchor Identifier Policy

## Document Control

- **Policy reference:** `POL-TA-2.0.0`
- **Revision:** 2.0.0
- **Status:** Effective
- **Published and effective:** September 10, 2026
- **Supersedes:** `POL-TA-1.0.0`
- **Superior policy:** `POL-PKI-2.0.0`
- **Registration authority:** Youngdo PKI Registration Authority

## 1. Scope

This policy governs individual trust-anchor identifiers under `1.3.6.1.4.1.65553.2.2`. The arc does not contain trust-anchor groups; groups and their versions are allocated under `1.3.6.1.4.1.65553.2.5`.

## 2. Identity Model

A trust anchor is identified by its X.500 name, SubjectPublicKeyInfo, key identifier, and applicable certification-path controls. A certificate may be recorded as a representation of the trust anchor, but a Subject distinguished name alone is not a unique cryptographic identity.

The identity-defining binding must include:

- the complete SubjectPublicKeyInfo in a stable binary format or an immutable artifact reference;
- a cryptographic digest with an explicitly named algorithm;
- the key identifier and its derivation or source;
- the trust-anchor name in canonical and human-readable forms;
- any policy, name, path-length, or usage constraints enforced by relying parties;
- supporting certificate artifacts, their digests, serial numbers, and validity periods, when certificates are used.

## 3. Allocation and Activation

Allocation and activation are separate decisions. A new OID may be reserved before a key ceremony, but its object state must remain `planned`. It may become `active` only after independent verification of the cryptographic binding and approval of the registration decision.

An assigned OID must never be rebound to a different public key. Rekeying requires a new OID. Reissuance of a certificate around the same public key may be added to the existing record only if the trust-anchor name and effective constraints remain unchanged.

## 4. Lifecycle

Permitted operational states are `unknown`, `planned`, `active`, `retired`, `compromised`, and `superseded`.

- `unknown` is used when the historic operational state cannot be substantiated.
- `planned` identifies a verified binding not yet placed into service.
- `active` requires a verified cryptographic binding.
- `retired` means no new deployment is intended, while historical references remain valid.
- `compromised` records an actual or suspected loss of key integrity.
- `superseded` links an old trust anchor to its replacement without erasing the old registration.

Removing trust from a relying-party store is an operational action; it does not delete or free the registered OID.

## 5. Legacy Records

OID values `.1` through `.13` were published before cryptographic binding fields were introduced. Decision `MIG-2026-0001` preserves those numeric assignments and their historic display names, but their bindings are marked `legacy-unverified` and operational state `unknown`.

These records must not be represented as verified or active until the Registration Authority has reconciled them against authoritative SubjectPublicKeyInfo, key identifiers, constraints, and certificate artifacts. Completing that reconciliation is a metadata-verification event; changing the public key would instead require a new OID.

## 6. Publication

The structured registry is authoritative for binding and lifecycle fields. Generated pages are informational projections. Each authoritative release must include a manifest covering the registry, schema, and exported views and must satisfy the signing requirements of `POL-ROOT-2.0.0`.

*End of Policy.*
